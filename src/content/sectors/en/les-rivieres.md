Les Rivières is one of the largest boroughs in the Communauté métropolitaine de Québec, and its
size is exactly why a sector-wide diagnosis is not very useful. One administration covers three
building stocks that have almost nothing in common.

## Three generations, none dominant

Along boulevard Wilfrid-Hamel in Vanier, older motels and pre-1980 apartment buildings line the
rivière Saint-Charles. Further west, Duberger-Les Saules is built up with bungalows and small
apartment buildings from another era. To the north, Lebourgneuf is the newest pocket: its condo
towers went up after boulevard Robert-Bourassa was extended in the 2000s, near rivière du
Berger. None of these three strata is large enough to pull the borough's picture toward it,
which is what caps the measured pressure at moderate rather than a clearer level.

## Vanier, a neighbourhood inside the borough

Vanier deserves to be named separately because it is often mistaken for the borough itself: it
is a neighbourhood of Les Rivières, not a synonym for it. The former town of Québec-Ouest,
absorbed by Québec in 2002, keeps its own drain profile along Wilfrid-Hamel, distinct from what
the borough-wide average shows. Its own sector page details what this Les Rivières hub can only
summarise.

## What the borough scale hides

Boulevard Pierre-Bertrand runs through all three pockets without touching any one of them long
enough to unify them, and place Fleur de Lys marks a commercial boundary more than a
geographic centre. For one specific address, the age of the building at that address matters
more than the borough's name on the tax bill.

## One observation that fits the address, not the borough

Note the date, the exact location and the conditions of each episode, then photograph it with a
ruler for scale. A camera inspection places the problem on your own section of pipe rather than
on a borough-wide average, and that observation, not the sector's name, should guide what comes
next.
