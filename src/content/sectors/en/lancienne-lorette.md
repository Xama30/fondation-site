L'Ancienne-Lorette holds a particular position in the region: a city of nearly 17,000 people
entirely surrounded by Québec, which chose in 2002 to remain a separate municipality rather than
merge. That administrative independence carries through to how a foundation problem gets
handled, since it is not the same city hall that answers.

## A tight cohort, with no river to make it worse

Between rue Notre-Dame and boulevard Wilfrid-Hamel, postwar bungalows and duplexes line up on
narrow lots, attached garage against attached garage, cedar hedge against cedar hedge. Nearly all
of that stock received the same clay tile drain between 1945 and 1975, and that cohort is now
reaching the age where joints pull apart. Unlike several neighbouring sectors built in the same
era along a river or a lake, no named body of water here keeps the table artificially high:
pressure depends mostly on the real condition of each drain section, not on a constant geographic
factor.

## An older core around the church, a different foundation entirely

The few streets around église Notre-Dame-de-l'Annonciation and rue Saint-Jean-Baptiste predate
the postwar boom. The buildings there rest on stone foundations, without a drain in the proper
sense, where the lime mortar between the fieldstones degrades under water. Treating this older
core as part of the neighbouring bungalow cohort leads to the wrong diagnosis, since the useful
step is not the same on both types of foundation.

## What the airport's proximity does and does not change

Near route de l'Aéroport and aéroport Jean-Lesage, the urban grid stays dense and sheds often sit
at the back of the lots. Nothing in the sector's public data links that proximity to the
condition of drains, and it is better not to invent a connection that is not established. What
matters is the age of the building stock, not the runway.

Whether the house is a postwar bungalow or a stone building near the church, the same rule
applies before committing to anything: date every episode of dampness, photograph it with a
ruler for scale, and insist on a proper inspection report. Checking an RBQ licence against the
registry rounds out that verification, here as elsewhere.
