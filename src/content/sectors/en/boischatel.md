Boischatel stretches east to west along two roads that share neither the same elevation nor the
same construction age: avenue Royale, on the plateau, and boulevard Sainte-Anne, at the foot of
the cliff overlooking fleuve Saint-Laurent. At the western edge of the municipality, that cliff
becomes dramatic at parc de la Chute-Montmorency, a landmark found nowhere else in the region.

## Two streets, two elevations, two drainage stories

Avenue Royale has occupied the top of the plateau since the earliest days of the colony; its
stone houses never had a drain to lose, and their upkeep remains a matter of mortar rather than
pipework. Boulevard Sainte-Anne, lower down, runs along fleuve Saint-Laurent on ground settled
later, where the slope toward the cliff naturally channels runoff toward foundations built at the
base of the escarpment. Two streets crossing the same municipality east to west, but not facing
the same water.

## What the waterfall adds to the west end

Near parc de la Chute-Montmorency, moisture carried by the waterfall's spray adds to ordinary
runoff on the properties closest to the cliff. This is not a factor touching all of Boischatel —
only the strip bordering the park — but it is something neither Beauport nor the municipalities
farther east share, since the waterfall itself exists nowhere else. A shed or a yard structure on
that strip feels the effect too, well before any water reaches the foundation.

## Before any excavation, find out where the water comes from

The useful reflex stays documentary: note where and when water appears, photograph it with a
ruler for scale, and note whether the foundation is stone or poured concrete, because the useful
next step is not the same for both. A professional inspection confirms the source before any work
begins, whether the property sits on boulevard Sainte-Anne, on avenue Royale, or in between, in
the wooded strip along the river.
