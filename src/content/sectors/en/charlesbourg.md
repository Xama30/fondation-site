Charlesbourg is not shaped like an ordinary borough: it is shaped like a fortified village that
was allowed to grow in concentric rings. That geometry, rare in Quebec City, splits the area into
two foundation logics that have little in common.

## A star-shaped village with no drain to lose

The Trait-carré, around avenue des Flandres and the old parish centre, keeps the star layout of
the fortified village founded in the seventeenth century. The houses bordering that core, often
stone or older masonry, never had a perimeter drain to lose: the technique did not exist when
they were built. What threatens their foundation is water passing through the mortar joints, not
a pipe collapsing underground.

## A belt built all at once, around Bourg-Royal and Orsainville

All around that core, out to Bourg-Royal, Orsainville and Notre-Dame-des-Laurentides, the
landscape changes completely: whole streets of bungalows raised between 1960 and 1985, on poured
concrete foundations connected to clay tile laid in short sections. These houses were built
quickly, often by the same contractor across several streets at once, which means their drains
age at the same rate and fail in the same decade.

## What rivière du Berger adds

The stretch along parc des Moulins drains toward rivière du Berger, a quiet tributary of rivière
Saint-Charles. As long as the clay tile network carries water normally, that slope changes
nothing day to day. The moment a run clogs or pulls apart, water sits against the foundation
longer than it would on flat ground with no outlet, and it is often there, near the park, that
the first signs appear before repeating further along boulevard Henri-Bourassa.

## Before trusting a quote

The distinction between the old core and the bungalow belt changes what a professional should
check first: the mortar on one side, the continuity of the drain on the other. The owner's role
stays the same in both cases — record, date, photograph with a ruler, and insist on a written
inspection report before authorising any excavation near Carrefour Charlesbourg or elsewhere in
the borough.
