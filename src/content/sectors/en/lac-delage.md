Lac-Delage is the smallest municipality on Québec City's north shore, and that size changes
how a foundation diagnosis even gets framed. Elsewhere, the central question is often "how
close is this house to the water?" Here, the answer is almost always the same: very close.

## A village built within a tight window

The earliest chalets date to the municipality's founding in 1959 and to the Manoir du
Lac-Delage's construction in 1963-1964. Unlike an area that spreads across several decades,
this founding cohort is compressed in time: most of the village's clay-tile drains were laid
within a few years of each other. The Manoir's 1976 expansion then brought a second, younger
wave of four-season condos between avenue du Lac and avenue des Monts.

## What the small territory changes

Across a little over two square kilometres around lac Delage, almost no lot sits far from the
water. That widespread proximity removes a variable that matters elsewhere on the north
shore: here, it is not a location on one street versus another that separates exposed
properties, since nearly the whole village is exposed. What separates one property from
another is the actual state of its drain — its construction cohort, its upkeep — not its
distance to the lake.

## Before authorising any work

On such a small territory, it is worth documenting each episode precisely before signing a
quote: the date, the exact location — crawl space, a wall near the woods, the area around the
outdoor spa — and a photo with a scale reference. A written inspection report lets you compare
two quotes against the same observations rather than two different pitches for houses that,
here more than elsewhere, often look a lot alike.
