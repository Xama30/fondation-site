Cap-Rouge sits at the western edge of the Sainte-Foy–Sillery–Cap-Rouge borough, at the mouth of
the rivière du Cap Rouge. It is a neighbourhood, not a municipality of its own: its pressure on
foundations reads at that precise scale, between the old core and the plateau above it.

## A village below the cliffs, a plateau above

The noyau villageois patrimonial runs along chemin du Roy, below le Tracel, the railway trestle
bridge completed in 1908 that remains the neighbourhood's visual landmark. The stone houses lining
that street predate 1945 and never had a perimeter drain. Higher up, set back from the vallée
boisée, an entire residential plateau was built between 1961 and 1990, on poured-concrete
footings connected, for the older half of that cohort, to clay drain tile.

## What the confluence adds to the problem

The rivière du Cap Rouge flows into the fleuve Saint-Laurent just below the plateau, under the
falaise de schiste rouge that gives the neighbourhood its name. That confluence holds runoff in
the narrow valley between le Tracel and the fleuve longer than land draining straight toward a
single watercourse would. A clay-tile drain nearing the end of its life then lets that water sit
against the footing far longer than on more open ground, such as around mont Bélair, at the other
end of the neighbourhood.

## A neighbourhood, not an isolated sector

Cap-Rouge also carries a héritage de poterie et de forge in its old core, evidence of a vanished
craft industry rather than a clue about current soil conditions. What actually separates a
property on chemin du Roy from one on the plateau is not that heritage but the century-plus gap
between the two building cohorts, and their respective position relative to the confluence.

Before authorising an excavation, the useful reflex stays documentary: date when a sign appeared,
photograph it with a ruler for scale, and demand a written inspection report that identifies the
cause rather than assuming it.
