The Sainte-Foy–Sillery–Cap-Rouge borough does not read like a single neighbourhood: it is an
administrative grouping of three formerly separate towns, each built in a different era and to a
different logic, before joining the city of Québec.

## Three towns, one borough

Sillery, Sainte-Foy and Cap-Rouge once existed as separate municipalities, each with its own
centre, its own pace of development and its own building stock. Sillery kept its large stone
estates on the falaise, Sainte-Foy largely rebuilt itself after the 2000s around Université Laval
and boulevard Laurier, and Cap-Rouge kept an old village core near chemin Saint-Louis, set back
from Vieux-Cap-Rouge. These three distinct histories now sit under a single borough name, but they
never merged into one uniform stock.

## Why no single figure works here

That absence of a dominant stratum is what caps the pressure level shown at the borough scale, for
both the french drain and foundation cracks. A figure averaged across three neighbourhoods flattens
both the most exposed properties on the falaise and the recent, lightly exposed stock around
Laurier Québec. The number shown here describes an administrative territory, not a building stock:
it is the Sillery, Sainte-Foy or Cap-Rouge page that carries the reading useful for a given
address.

## What still ties the three neighbourhoods together

The promenade Samuel-De Champlain runs along the river from the falaise in Sillery to
Vieux-Cap-Rouge, crossing all three former territories without stopping at their historical
borders. It's a useful reminder that these neighbourhoods stay close geographically even where
their building stock differs sharply: student housing near Université Laval, a century-old villa
on avenue Maguire, and a heritage house in the Cap-Rouge village core share the same borough, the
same 311 line, but not the same footing.

Before authorising any work, whichever neighbourhood is involved, the starting point stays the
same: date a sign, photograph it with a ruler, and demand a written inspection report before any
excavation.
