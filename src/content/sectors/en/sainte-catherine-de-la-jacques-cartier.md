Rivière Jacques-Cartier crosses the municipality from north to south, and it is the river, far
more than the year a house was built, that draws the map of damp basements in
Sainte-Catherine-de-la-Jacques-Cartier.

## The riverfront row, not the age of the foundation

Along chemin de la Liseuse, which follows the bank through a riverfront woodlot, houses often sit on foundations as recent as
those of the residential belt built after 2005 along route 367, with the same drain built to
standard BNQ 3661-500. The difference does not come from the foundation: it comes from the ground
around it. Rivière Jacques-Cartier recharges the water table continuously along that riverfront
strip, and a drain working perfectly still does not stop the adjoining ground from staying
saturated for a good part of the year.

## A recent belt, out of reach

A few streets back from the river, in the residential belt bordering route 367, that same
generation of poured concrete has nothing extra to shed: no river nearby, no water table held
artificially high. The concrete there is still too young to show shrinkage, and the drain is more
than equal to the task it was laid for.

## The village core, a different mechanism

The historic village core, set back from the river, rests on stone foundations that never
received a drain, regardless of how close they sit to the water. What degrades there is the
mortar between the stones under ground moisture passing through the wall — a mechanism with
nothing in common with a drain saturated by a river, even if the visible symptom, a basement that
smells damp, can look identical at first glance.
