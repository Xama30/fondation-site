Lebourgneuf stands apart from most neighbouring sectors on a single fact: almost all of its
building stock dates from after 2005. No clay tile nearing the end of its life, no undrained
stone masonry — a rare case across the territory, and one that calls for a different explanation.

## A sector born after today's standards

Quartier Mosaïque, at the junction of autoroute Robert-Bourassa and boulevard Lebourgneuf, and the
LB9 towers near autoroute Laurentienne, have been built since the 2010s to the BNQ 3661-500
standard now in force: new PVC drains, recent poured concrete. That cohort dominates the sector's
data and explains a low pressure level, for both the french drain and foundation cracks.

## What a young sector does not remove

Recent construction is not risk-free construction, only a different kind of risk. Where an older
neighbourhood faces a material aging out, a recent residential build like the one on rue des
Métis can instead reveal a construction defect — a poorly sealed joint, out-of-spec concrete
shrinkage. That kind of problem usually falls within the period covered by the mandatory GCR
new-home warranty plan for residential construction in Québec, which changes the first step to
take: check the warranty before ordering an excavation.

## Towers and big-box retail: a foundation that isn't individual

Around the Mega Centre Lebourgneuf big-box retail cluster, most of the residential stock takes the
form of rental condo towers, where the foundation and utility shafts are shared across several
units rather than tied to a single address. A problem found in one of these towers usually
involves the whole condo corporation, not one owner, which sets Lebourgneuf clearly apart from a
single-family neighbourhood.

Before authorising anything, the reflex stays the same as elsewhere: date a sign, photograph it
with a ruler, and first check the warranty coverage that applies to construction this recent.
