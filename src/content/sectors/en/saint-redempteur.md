Saint-Rédempteur existed as a municipality in its own right for more than eighty years before
becoming a Lévis sector, and that administrative history explains why its original village fits
into such a small footprint against the rest of the building stock. The sector's name survived
the merger; the scale of its building stock was redefined by a single, far more recent wave of
construction.

## A village that vanished inside its own sector

Founded in 1919, the former municipality of Saint-Rédempteur was built around the église
Très-Saint-Rédempteur and the Canadian National gare Chaudière, on the west bank of rivière
Chaudière. The 2002 municipal merger erased the administrative border, but not the imbalance in
the building stock: that original core now occupies only a fraction of the commercial and
residential sector stretching along route des Rivières and rue de Saint-Denis, around the Place
St-Rédempteur shopping centre. A corner grocery store and several service businesses now occupy
that commercial heart, just a few streets from the remains of the village founded in 1919.

## Fill sloping toward the river

Most houses in the sector date from 1975 to 1990 and sit on concrete footings poured directly
onto cultivated-land fill sloping toward rivière Chaudière. The corrugated PVC pipe ringing
these foundations crushes and deforms at the joints faster than it would on flat ground, while
shrinkage from concrete poured in a single season already marks the basement walls of several
streets in secteur Bernières.

## What that means for a diagnosis

A shrinkage crack on a poured wall from that cohort does not share a cause with an infiltration
near the old village. Note the exact location, the date and the weather conditions of each
episode, photograph it with a ruler for scale, and insist on an inspection that tells fill
settlement apart from a simply clogged drain before authorising any work. The inspection report
also lets you compare several quotes against the same observation, rather than against
different sales pitches from one contractor to the next.
