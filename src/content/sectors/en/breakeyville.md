Breakeyville is the smallest of the areas along rivière Chaudière on the Lévis side, and its
size is exactly what misleads people: it looks uniform because it fits into a few streets, when
in fact it layers two ways of building separated by a century and a half.

## A village born from a mill

The core formed around the sawmill Hans Denaston Breakey had built in 1846 on rivière Chaudière.
The houses that grew along rue Saint-Augustin and rue Sainte-Hélène mostly predate 1945 and sit
on fieldstone foundations with no perimeter drain: the technique was not widespread when they
went up. Groundwater passes through the wall and attacks the lime mortar rather than collapsing
a pipe that does not exist.

Today the sawmill is a ruin skirted by the Éco-Parc de la Chaudière cycling path, extended by
Route verte no 6 toward parc des Fougères and parc Ian-Breakey. The route remains useful for
locating the old core: everything directly bordering the river and the mill remains belongs to
that first building stock.

## What changed further out, toward the highway

A newer fringe, built after 2005 toward autoroute 73, belongs to an entirely different register:
poured concrete, PVC drain laid to standard BNQ 3661-500. Nothing in common with the foundations
of the village core. It is one of the few areas where the recent stock shows low pressure on both
services while the historic core, a few streets away, shows the highest possible level on the
drain.

## Before committing to anything

The starting point differs depending on whether you live near rue Saint-Augustin or out by the
highway. Note the exact location of an infiltration, the date and the weather conditions,
photograph it with a ruler for scale, and have an inspection confirm whether the problem comes
from the mortar of an old stone foundation or from a younger drain that has simply stopped
working.
