On most sectors around the Communauté métropolitaine, one question settles almost everything:
which side does the water come from? On Île d'Orléans, the question does not work the same way,
because the fleuve Saint-Laurent does not have a single side here: it surrounds the territory
entirely, and the six municipalities linked by chemin Royal share the same ancestral stone
building stock, with no original drain, but not the same way of facing it.

## One stone, two different shores

Chemin Royal circles the whole island, from Sainte-Pétronille at the western tip to
Saint-François-de-l'Île-d'Orléans at the eastern one. Houses built in rubble stone before 1945
stand on both shores, but the north shore, open to the wide river toward
Sainte-Famille-de-l'Île-d'Orléans, takes on a fetch and a swell that the south shore, more
sheltered facing Beauport and the pont de l'Île d'Orléans, does not carry with the same
intensity. An identical foundation does not take the same pressure depending on which channel
it faces.

## Crawl spaces, orchards and barns

Part of the island's ancestral stock sits on a crawl space rather than a fully excavated
basement, a difference that changes how a problem shows up. Where a basement shows a puddle at
the base of a wall, a crawl space instead holds a persistent musty smell and, over time, sill
wood that rots without being noticed directly from the living space above. Around these houses,
the orchards and vineyards that give the island its agricultural character also surround barns
and outbuildings raised in the same period, on the same kind of foundation — a building stock
that the single word "house" does not cover.

## Before calling in a professional

The island has only one road access, the pont de l'Île d'Orléans on the Beauport side. That
changes nothing about the useful gesture, but it is worth gathering observations before a first
visit rather than after. Date the sign as it appears, photograph it with a ruler for scale, and
note whether it is a smell, a stain or wood giving way: that distinction points toward a very
different diagnosis depending on whether the structure is an ancestral crawl space or a newer
basement. Then insist on a written inspection report, and check the RBQ licence of any
contractor consulted against the official registry before signing a quote.
