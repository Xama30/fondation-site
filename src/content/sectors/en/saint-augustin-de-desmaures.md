Saint-Augustin-de-Desmaures refused the 2002 municipal merger and remains a distinct city of a
little over 20,000 people, squeezed between fleuve Saint-Laurent and rivière du Cap-Rouge. That
geographic position, more than population growth alone, explains why two very different building
stocks sit side by side here.

## Les Bocages: new, but not immune

In the Les Bocages sector and around lac Saint-Augustin, wood-frame cottages from the last
century have largely given way, since the 2000s, to new single-family homes on poured-concrete
foundations with drains meeting the BNQ 3661-500 standard. Material compliance does not remove a
separate factor, though: several of these builds sit on fill from former vacation lots, between
the lake and rivière du Cap-Rouge, where the water table stays close to the surface even with a
newer drain.

## The village core along chemin du Roy, a different century

Along chemin du Roy and route 138, the older village core rests on stone foundations, with no
original drain. Groundwater there passes through the wall rather than collapsing a pipe, and it
is the mortar between the fieldstones that shows the wear. Mistaking this core for the newer
development in Les Bocages means chasing the wrong cause.

## Squeezed between the river and the waterway

The city's position, wedged between fleuve Saint-Laurent and rivière du Cap-Rouge, places much
of its territory in a band where two waterways compete for natural drainage. This named,
documented geography explains a water table that stays high in places — it says nothing about
soil composition, which is not established for this sector.

Between a new foundation laid on fill and a century-old stone wall, only an inspection report
truly tells the two causes apart. The sound practice is to date every episode, photograph it
with a ruler for scale, and keep that report on hand to compare two quotes against the same
facts rather than two sales pitches.
