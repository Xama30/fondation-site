Lévis is not a sector, it is a city that absorbed thirteen municipalities in a little over
twenty years, from Saint-Nicolas to Pintendre. The size of the territory hides a simple but
decisive difference: what threatens a footing on the plateau is not what threatens one in
Vieux-Lévis, down by the river.

## A plateau built all at once, an older core by the river

The Terrasse de Lévis, the vast plateau overlooking the fleuve Saint-Laurent, filled up with
bungalows and single-family homes between 1970 and 2000, with an attached garage and a finished
basement nearly everywhere. The till carrying that plateau drains well, which is why the
perimeter drain from that generation tends to hold up. Vieux-Lévis, on lower ground near the
traverse Québec-Lévis, keeps an older housing stock where pressure from the nearby river is
felt more.

## The weak point is not the drain, it is the garage

On the plateau, the most common symptom is not a puddle in the basement but a crack starting at
the corner where the attached garage meets the main footing. Those two sections of poured
concrete, laid at different points in the build, each move at their own pace, and it is that
joint, repeated house after house along the boulevard Guillaume-Couture corridor, that gives
way first. It is not a sign of soil moisture: it is a design point that repeats everywhere this
construction method was used.

## A territory with thirteen faces, one habit before signing

Saint-Romuald, Charny, Saint-Nicolas, Lauzon, Pintendre: each of these areas has its own
construction pattern, tied to the rest of the city by the autoroute Jean-Lesage and served by
the business hub that grew up near the Cité Desjardins de la coopération. Treating Lévis as one
uniform block misses that diversity, and it matters when comparing two quotes. The useful habit
stays the same everywhere, though: photograph the crack with a ruler, note the date and whether
it changes with the seasons, and insist on a written inspection report before authorising an
excavation. On the plateau as in Vieux-Lévis, that report lets you compare two quotes against
the same observation rather than two different pitches.
