Duberger–Les Saules is worth distinguishing from the borough that contains it, Les Rivières: it
is a specific neighbourhood, made up of five distinct residential pockets and an industrial
park that borders them without blending in, with its own construction history.

## A neighbourhood born of a cooperative, not a developer

Between 1950 and 1975, the cooperative movement gave families in Duberger and Les Saules a path
to ownership outside the usual developer model. The homes of Place-Duclos and
Domaine-Saint-Charles, built through that movement, sit on poured-concrete footings connected to
clay tile laid at the same time. That cohort, built collectively rather than house by house,
aged as a block, and it is now reaching the end of that drain type's useful life.

## An industrial park that borders without blending in

The parc industriel Duberger runs along avenue Saint-Sacrement and boulevard Wilfrid-Hamel,
with its distribution warehouses and loading docks. It sits next to the residential pockets
without overlapping them: Duberger, Les Saules, Place-Duclos and Domaine-Saint-Charles remain
separate residential pockets, linked to each other by boulevard Pierre-Bertrand rather than by
the industrial park itself.

## A footing poured to the same standard, before authorising any work

This may be the neighbourhood's most distinctive trait: the cooperative build's footings were
poured to uniform methods, lot after lot, rather than by different crews each working their own
way as is often the case elsewhere. The result is limited shrinkage cracking, with little
variation from one house to the next — a more predictable foundation profile than average, even
though the clay-tile drain from that cohort is itself reaching the end of the line. The owner's
reflex stays the same as anywhere else: note the location and date of a leak, photograph it with
a ruler, and insist on a written inspection report before any excavation. In Duberger–Les
Saules, that report also helps tell a problem specific to the cooperative generation apart from
one of more recent origin, which is not always obvious to the eye.
