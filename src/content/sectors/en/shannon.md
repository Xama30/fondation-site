Shannon stands apart from other municipalities on the north shore because of its history: the
community exists in its current form largely because the neighbouring military base became
permanent, and that history still reads in the age of the sector's foundations.

## A subdivision born of a specific moment

Valcartier base became a permanent installation in the early 1950s, and Shannon's first
civilian subdivision grew up around that moment, along boulevard Jacques-Cartier and chemin de
Gosford. Unlike a sector built in waves spread across several decades, this founding cohort is
concentrated in time, with a clay-tile drain in short sections that is now close to the end of
its service life.

## What a private well reveals

Several homes on the sector's large rural lots are still served by a private well rather than
the municipal network. That detail, often treated purely as a drinking-water matter, is also a
construction clue: it generally signals a foundation laid before the network arrived, so
closer to the 1950s founding cohort than to the sector's more recent construction.

## What the river adds

Lots closest to rivière Jacques-Cartier, next to the base's wooded training grounds,
experience a water table that swings more from season to season than the rest of the
territory. That swing stays invisible as long as the drain works; it becomes more apparent in a
basement the moment the drain starts to weaken.

## Before authorising any work

In a territory where a foundation's age tracks proximity to the municipal network as much as
the street it sits on, it is worth having an inspection confirm the water's exact source. Note
whether your home has a private well, document each episode with a date and a photo for scale,
and insist on a written report before authorising any excavation.
