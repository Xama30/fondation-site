A buried foundation wall is only ever visible at two moments: when it is built, and whenever an
excavation exposes it again. In between, nobody really knows what state the barrier protecting it
is in — and that is what makes waterproofing different from the other services on this list. The
decision does not get made when a problem shows up inside, because by then the wall has been
backfilled for years. It gets made during the few days a trench happens to be open for another
reason — most often a French drain replacement.

## A window that opens once, not twice

Excavating down to the footing exposes the wall along its entire buried height, for a matter of
days before backfill goes back in. A wall that has not been reopened since 1990 usually still
carries a tar coating applied at the time of construction — protection against surface moisture
while the concrete cured, not a membrane built to hold back groundwater pressure for decades.
Not acting while the wall happens to be exposed means backfilling a foundation in the same state
it was found, already knowing it will not hold.

## What separates a coating from a membrane

The difference is not the thickness of the layer, it is what the layer is supposed to do once the
backfill goes back in. A coating protects the concrete while it cures; a membrane is built to stay
watertight under continuous groundwater pressure, year after year. A drainage board installed
against the membrane keeps that pressure from bearing on it directly. A quote that says
"waterproofing" without saying whether the board is included does not necessarily describe the
same system as the next one — two quotes that look comparable can cover two very different jobs.

## What a quote needs to itemise

Check that the quote separates the two scopes — drain and waterproofing — even when both are done
in the same job. A single lump sum that does not split them hides what is actually included, and
more importantly what is not if you decide to put one of the two off. That delay has a specific
cost: reopening the same trench a second time, for one scope only, a few years later.
