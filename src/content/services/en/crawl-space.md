A crawl space is not a space sealed off in on itself. It is a volume of air in constant contact
with the floor framing it supports, and that air does not stay down there: it migrates toward the
living space through every path a house offers — registers, joints, pipe penetrations. Whatever
happens down there never stays confined to the space itself, which is what sets this service apart
from the other water and moisture subjects on this list: here it is the framing itself at stake,
not just the wall or the slab.

## The air does not stay down there

A smell rising through the registers, a cold floor in winter above the crawl space — both signs
say the same thing: air from that space moves freely toward the rest of the house. A bare earth
floor, with nothing slowing the moisture exchange with the surrounding air, keeps that movement
going year-round, whether the house is occupied or not.

## Sealing it up is not the same as fixing it

The most common mistake is treating a crawl space as something to hide rather than a problem to
understand. Insulating and sealing an access point without first addressing where the ground
moisture comes from locks that moisture in with the framing, out of sight, where it keeps working
with no sign left to warn anyone. Wood that has started to darken or stain around the crawl space
perimeter does not stop darkening because the access is sealed.

## What a contractor documents before stepping in

Before any work, the condition of the ground, the framing, and the moisture level all need to be
established, because the range of possible work is wide — from a ground vapour barrier to
perimeter insulation, by way of managing the water that collects there. These are different jobs
depending on what is actually at fault, and mixing them up risks treating a visible symptom while
its cause keeps working under the floor.

A crawl space also happens to be one of the few places in the house where the framing's condition
is directly observable. Photographing the darkened or stained wood, and noting where along the
perimeter it sits, gives a contractor a precise starting point rather than a rough description
from memory.
