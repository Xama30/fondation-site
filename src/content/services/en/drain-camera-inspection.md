Between a cleanout costing a few hundred dollars and an excavation costing several thousand sits
one observation that is missing nine times out of ten: nobody looked inside the drain before
proposing a fix. A camera inspection exists to close exactly that gap, and it is what decides
which side of that cost range a job lands on.

## What a camera shows, and what it does not

A camera fed into the drain through an existing access point reveals what no guess from outside
ever confirms: soil clogging, roots that have pierced a joint, an iron ochre deposit, or on the
other hand a pipe that is physically crushed, pulled apart, or sagging along a section. The first
group can sometimes be dealt with without digging; the second never can. What the camera does not
do is explain why the ground moved under a sagging pipe — that question sits outside the scope of
an inspection and belongs to a broader diagnosis.

## The recording is the deliverable, not the phone call

A contractor who describes what they saw over the phone, without handing over the footage, turns
a verifiable observation into an opinion you have to take on faith. The recording and the written
report that comes with it are what belong to you once the inspection is done, and they are what
let you compare two replacement quotes against the same footage instead of two competing sales
pitches. A replacement quote offered without that footage is proposing a solution before it has
shown the problem.

## Three moments to ask for one

Before accepting a replacement quote, to confirm the excavation being proposed is really the only
option. Before buying a house whose drain condition appears nowhere in the available paperwork,
because a drain near the end of its life often shows no visible sign from inside before the first
leak. And the moment a freshly cleaned drain clogs a second time within a short span, which almost
always points to something other than an ordinary blockage — iron ochre among the possibilities.
In all three cases, the inspection produces a dated record you keep, regardless of what is
decided next — cleaning, monitoring, or excavation. It is that record, not the memory of a
conversation, that becomes the point of comparison if the same drain comes up again the following
year.
