On most combinations in this list, the question put to a homeowner is whether to replace a
clay-tile drain nearing the end of its life. On Île d'Orléans, the question is different: the
ancestral rubble-stone building stock along chemin Royal, set in lime mortar before 1945, never
had an original french drain to wear out. Installing a first drain against a wall of that kind is
not the same decision as reopening a trench around a poured-concrete footing.

## A wall that has never been excavated against

Digging along a rubble-stone wall to lay a new drain against it touches a structure that lime
mortar was never built to withstand. Before accepting a quote, an owner of an ancestral chemin
Royal house should have the wall itself assessed, not just the planned run of the drain, since
excavation exposes joints that have never faced this kind of prolonged contact with air and
surface water. That is an order of verification, not an order of works: a written opinion on the
wall before the trench opens costs far less than discovering a problem once the foundation is
already exposed.

## A permit that depends on the municipality, not one 311 line

Île d'Orléans has no single municipal administration. The six constituent municipalities, from
Sainte-Pétronille to Sainte-Famille-de-l'Île-d'Orléans, each issue their own excavation permits.
A quote that plans work near chemin Royal or a drainage ditch should state which municipality was
contacted, since a confirmation obtained in Saint-Jean-de-l'Île-d'Orléans does not apply to a
neighbouring lot under a different administration.

## What narrow lots change about access

Chemin Royal lots inherit the seigneurial survey's narrow strips, laid out perpendicular to the
fleuve Saint-Laurent: the house often sits at the front of a long, tight lot, with an orchard or
vineyard starting right behind it, sometimes a barn further back on the same strip of land. Room
to manoeuvre around the foundation is therefore tighter than on a suburban lot, and protection of
the cultivated rows during the work is worth spelling out in the quote rather than discovered on
the day the crew arrives. Contractors who regularly serve the island are also fewer, which can
lengthen the wait before comparing more than one quote for the same crawl space or the same stone
wall.
