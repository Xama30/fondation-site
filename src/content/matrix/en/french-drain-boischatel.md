In Boischatel, the french drain that actually wears out is not the one people picture first.
Avenue Royale, running along the top of the plateau with its stone houses predating 1945, never
had a drain: its movement shows up in the mortar, not in a pipe. The cohort driving a
replacement quote sits farther south, in the street grid that grew up around the parish church
built in 1937 and was later extended west — homes built almost entirely within the same
quarter-century, carrying the same clay-tile drain now at the end of its life.

## One street asks a different question than the next

A house near the streets extended west of the church raises a pipe question: the clay-tile
sections have pulled apart, filled with sediment, and stopped carrying water. A house on avenue
Royale raises a mortar question. Comparing the two quotes as if they answered the same problem
leads to misjudging one of them — and a contractor who proposes a drain replacement on a stone
foundation without first checking its age deserves to be asked again.

## The base of the cliff takes on more water than the plateau

Boischatel's ground slopes toward the fleuve Saint-Laurent, and that grade concentrates runoff
against foundations built at the bottom of the slope, on the boulevard Sainte-Anne side. A new
drain there works harder than an equivalent drain up on the plateau. At the western edge, near
parc de la Chute-Montmorency, moisture carried by mist from the falls adds to that runoff for
properties closest to the wooded riverside strip along the river — a factor neither the rest of
Boischatel nor the neighbouring municipalities share.

## What the quote should spell out before you sign

A camera inspection report distinguishing a clogged drain from a crushed one, the standard and
pipe type named, the position of the Municipalité de Boischatel's urban planning department on
the permit, and a line on what moves before excavation starts — a shed, a woodpile, any
landscaping against the foundation. Four verifiable items, none of which depends on trust
extended in advance.
