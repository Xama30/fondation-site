On most sectors, the first question in a drain quote is distance to the water. In Lac-Delage,
that question sorts almost nothing: the village covers a little over two square kilometres, and
both avenue du Lac and avenue des Monts sit within walking distance of lac Delage.

## Comparing quotes without distance to the lake as a marker

Elsewhere, an owner compares quotes partly on how close the house sits to the water. Here, that
marker nearly disappears: what should carry the comparison is the construction cohort named in
the quote — a chalet from the village's 1959 founding, or a condo from the wave the Manoir's
1976 expansion brought — and the drain's condition as established by an inspection rather than a
visual estimate.

## What varies is not the street but what borders the foundation

On a territory this small, the difference between two jobs does not come from avenue du Lac
versus avenue des Monts, but from what touches the foundation directly: a dense woodlot limiting
access on one side, an outdoor spa or a fixed feature occupying it on the other. Have the planned
access, and what will need to be moved, spelled out before accepting a quote rather than
discovered the day the crew arrives. That single variable — what sits against the wall, not
which street the address is on — does more to explain two different-looking quotes here than
anything a distance-to-water column could show.

## Manoir condos add a step

On condo units near the Manoir du Lac-Delage, part of the 1976 wave, site access can depend on
an agreement among co-owners rather than a single owner's call. That administrative step, more
specific to condos than to individual chalets, is worth settling before setting a schedule —
otherwise an otherwise complete quote sits waiting on a collective signature rather than on the
excavation work itself.
