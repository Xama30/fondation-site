On a serviced urban lot, replacing a drain usually comes down to a question of access around the
foundation. In Shannon, the question widens: a large share of the territory is still served by a
private well and a septic field rather than a municipal network, which adds something to locate
before excavation is even on the table.

## What a private well changes before digging starts

On a large rural lot along boulevard Jacques-Cartier or chemin de Gosford, a private well and its
septic field occupy part of the property that a drain excavation has to respect. The distance
between the well, the septic field, and the planned discharge point for the new drain needs to be
settled before excavation begins, not once the excavator has arrived. An owner who does not know
exactly where these sit should have them located before asking for a priced quote.

## A large lot, but a community worth addressing in its own language

Rural properties in the area generally offer generous clearance around the foundation, unlike a
tight urban lot: the limit rarely comes from available space. It comes instead from what the
property already holds — a well, a septic field, a property line against a neighbouring woodlot.
Shannon is also an English-speaking community, and that is not incidental here: the quote, the
permit confirmation, or the exchange with the municipality's citizen services can happen in
English, and it is worth stating that on the first call rather than discovering it partway
through.

## The edge of the base's woodlot

On lots that border Valcartier base's wooded training grounds, near rivière Jacques-Cartier, the
exact property line is worth confirming before excavation gets close to the treeline. That is a
boundary check separate from the drain diagnosis, but it keeps a crawl space or shed project from
spilling onto land the owner does not hold title to.
